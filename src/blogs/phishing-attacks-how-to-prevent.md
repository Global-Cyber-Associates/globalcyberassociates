# Phishing Attacks: How to Recognize and Prevent Them

Phishing is the **starting point of most cyberattacks**. Before ransomware encrypts files or an attacker steals data, there's usually a phishing message that tricked someone into clicking, logging in, or replying.

The good news: once you know the patterns, phishing becomes far easier to spot and stop.

---

## What Is Phishing?

Phishing is a form of **social engineering** — manipulating people rather than hacking machines. Attackers impersonate someone you trust (a bank, a colleague, a vendor, your CEO) to trick you into:

- Revealing passwords or one-time codes
- Clicking a malicious link
- Opening an infected attachment
- Transferring money or sensitive data

---

## Common Types of Phishing

- **Email phishing** — mass fake emails impersonating trusted brands.
- **Spear phishing** — targeted, personalised attacks on specific people.
- **Whaling** — spear phishing aimed at executives.
- **Smishing** — phishing via SMS/text messages.
- **Vishing** — voice phishing over phone calls.
- **Business Email Compromise (BEC)** — impersonating an executive or vendor to authorise fraudulent payments.

---

## Red Flags: How to Spot a Phishing Attempt

Train yourself and your team to pause when you see:

- ⚠️ **Urgency or fear** — "Your account will be closed in 24 hours!"
- 🔗 **Mismatched links** — hover before clicking; the real URL doesn't match the text.
- ✉️ **Look-alike sender addresses** — `support@paypa1.com` instead of `paypal.com`.
- 📎 **Unexpected attachments** — especially `.zip`, `.exe`, or macro-enabled documents.
- 🙋 **Requests for credentials or codes** — legitimate companies don't ask for your password.
- 💸 **Unusual payment or gift-card requests** — a classic BEC tactic.
- 🧩 **Generic greetings** — "Dear Customer" instead of your name.

> **Golden rule:** when in doubt, verify through a *separate, known channel* — don't reply to the suspicious message itself.

---

## How to Prevent Phishing

### For Individuals
- Enable **multi-factor authentication (MFA)** — even if a password is stolen, attackers are blocked.
- Never click links in unexpected messages; type the address yourself.
- Keep software and browsers updated.
- Report suspicious emails instead of deleting them silently.

### For Businesses
- **Train employees regularly** with real-world phishing simulations.
- Deploy **email filtering** and anti-spoofing (SPF, DKIM, DMARC).
- Enforce **MFA** on all accounts, especially email and remote access.
- **Monitor endpoints** for what happens *after* a click — early detection limits damage.
- Establish a **clear reporting process** so staff know exactly what to do.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What should I do if I clicked a phishing link?**
Disconnect from the network, change the affected password from a clean device, enable MFA, and report it to your IT/security team immediately.

**Can phishing be fully prevented?**
No tool stops 100% of attempts — but MFA, training, email filtering, and monitoring together reduce both the success rate and the damage dramatically.

**Why do attackers target employees instead of systems?**
People are often the easiest path in. It's far simpler to trick someone than to break strong technical controls.
